**I build the data infrastructure that connects AI agents to premium, rights-respecting knowledge sources.**

I'm Head of AI Solutions at [Alien Intelligence](https://alien.club). My job is to make the world's most valuable knowledge — scientific research, cultural heritage, rights-managed media — usable by AI agents *on the source's terms*: rights preserved, provenance intact, institutions credited and compensated.

Three projects carry that work today:

- [**OpenAIRE MCP**](/case-studies/openaire-mcp/) — connecting AI agents to the OpenAIRE Research Graph and its 150M+ scientific records through the Model Context Protocol.
- [**Gallica / BnF**](/case-studies/gallica-bnf/) — a multi-year partnership with the Bibliothèque nationale de France making digitised heritage consumable by AI agents, on the library's terms.
- [**LDS / Copyfair**](/case-studies/lds-copyfair/) — contract-first data infrastructure that preserves copyright and provenance when AI systems ingest rights-holders' content.

## Why this, why me

This is less a pivot than a continuation. I spent eighteen years building shared data infrastructure for mobility: [open standards for Mobility-as-a-Service in France](/case-studies/maas-standards/), [moB — Mon Compte Mobilité](/case-studies/mob/), the [30 LEV field trial](/case-studies/30-lev/) — always with the same cast of cities, ministries, operators and startups who needed data to flow between organisations without anyone being locked in or strip-mined.

That is exactly the problem AI has now. Agents need access to high-quality knowledge; the institutions that hold it need contracts, attribution and revenue — not bulk scraping. The discipline I learned in mobility — open standards, multi-stakeholder governance, infrastructure that aligns public missions with private incentives — is the discipline this moment needs.
